# Animo

**Configurable actuators for cheap, printable toys and models — by Motcore.**

Animo is the small line of the Motcore family. Instead of wiring a servo into
every new design, you build a **Motcore** once — which faces carry a motor,
which are blank, how many bricks — and then snap printed models, the
**Coremata**, onto it: a puppet, a crane, a walker, a board game. One
Motcore, many models.

```
face  →  brick  →  Motcore  →  Coremata
```

- **Face**: one side's module — a motor, a blank, later a screen or a sensor. The
  unit you swap.
- **Brick**: the frame, a 72 mm cube that four faces slide into.
- **Motcore**: one or more bricks with their faces — a configuration.
- **Coremata**: the model that goes on top.

(Working names; the product names may change.)

> Status: **early prototype**. Faces, gears and test models of earlier
> versions have been printed and run; the current design (below) is in CAD,
> checked for interference, not printed yet.

## The brick

- **72 mm (9 LEGO modules)**: two identical decks with their corner columns,
  four windows. Each face slides out through its window without opening the
  brick, held by two M2 countersunk screws to bosses on the decks.
- Inside: battery (1S LiPo) over the motors, the brain's boards under them.
- No LEGO on the motor faces; the brick keeps the LEGO grid, and LEGO comes
  back as a face of its own.

## The motor faces

- **Types**: a **worm N20** lying flat on its plate (the one being built), a
  straight **N20**, a continuous **MG90D** (set aside). They share one standard, so most of them mix in one
  brick.
- **Double swing-gear clutch**: the motor's pinion drags an arm with two
  swing gears; turning one way swings one gear into mesh with the output,
  the other way the other one. Parked in the middle, both stand clear and
  the **output turns free** with the motor stopped — what a walker with
  passive dynamics needs. The firmware parks it, reading the output's angle
  on an **AS5600** through a 1:1 sensor gear.
- **Output on a ball bearing** (6700ZZ) in a 5.6 mm plate, flush; hub and
  gear joined by one M3 from inside.
- **The Coremata's coupling**: a **trapezoid socket** (a square with one side
  shorter) in the hub — one position only, four flat sides for the torque.
  It only turns the part: holding it on is the Coremata's job, from its own
  grip on the brick. `ModelKey` is the model side's template part.

## Files

| File | What |
|------|------|
| `cad/animo_brick.py` | FreeCAD macro: the brick (frame) and which face goes on which side (`CUBE` at the top); checks it all headless. `ANIMO_MIX=1` tries every face type next to every other |
| `cad/animo_face_n20_worm.py` | Face with a worm N20 lying flat on its plate (the one being built). Opens a brick with four of them and checks it |
| `cad/animo_face_n20.py` | Face with a straight N20 gearmotor |
| `cad/animo_face_mg90d.py` | Face with a continuous MG90D — set aside: no room behind its AS5600 for the wires |
| `cad/animo_lib.py` | What every face shares: sizes, output, coupling, swing gears, sensor, frame, checks (not a macro) |
| `cad/stl/<file>/PETG/` | Printable parts, regenerated by each macro (`ANIMO_MATERIAL=PLA` headless for a PLA set) |
| `cad/animo_fit_coupon.py` | Fit coupons. STLs in `cad/stl/coupon/` |
| `cad/archive/` | Earlier designs, v1 to v5, kept for reference |

Run any macro headless to read its checks, e.g.
`"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.0/bin/freecadcmd.exe" animo_brick.py`.

### Printer notes (Creality Hi, Creality Print, 0.4 nozzle)

Turn **arc moves off** (small circles came out 0.2 mm oval) and use a **scarf
seam** (the plain seam's ~0.2 mm bump made fits scatter). Measured with those,
in PETG on a 6 mm feature: holes print ~0.27 small, outsides ~0.17 small;
press fit +0.05, sliding fit +0.25 (drawn hole minus drawn pin).
