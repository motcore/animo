# Animo

**A configurable actuator cube for cheap, printable toys and models — by Motcore.**

Animo is the small line of the Motcore family. Instead of wiring a servo into
every new design, you configure a cube once — which walls carry a motor, which
are blank, what sits on top — and then snap printed models onto it: a
puppet, a crane, a walker, a board game. One configuration, many models.

> Status: **early design**. Nothing here has been printed yet.

## The cube (v1)

- **57.9 mm cube**, four identical side walls, each a self-contained module.
- Each motor wall holds an **MG90D** (position) or an **MG90S 360°**
  (continuous) — same body, same module. A blank wall is the same plate
  without the servo.
- The four servos stand upright in a **pinwheel** round the cube's centre,
  leaving a square hole through the middle for the wiring.
- Each servo drives its wall's output through **two equal spur gears (1:1)**,
  so the output is on the **centre of the wall** and keeps the servo's whole
  travel.
- **Flat faces.** The output is a **female D socket** flush with the wall; the
  model brings the male D (one way in, so the servo's zero is known). Four
  **flush magnets** hold the model on.
- No bearings, no steel shafts: the output hub turns in a plain hole and is
  captured by the wall alone.
- Walls are trapped between two **identical decks** by tongue and groove; four
  M3×10 countersunk screws close the cube.
- The **brain** is a half cube that replaces the top deck: ESP32-C3 SuperMini,
  TP4056 USB-C charger, 5 V boost, switch and a 1S LiPo. Cube + brain stand
  1.5 cubes tall.

## Files

| File | What |
|------|------|
| `cad/animo_v1_cube.py` | FreeCAD macro: the whole cube, parametric, with its own interference checks. Run it headless to read them: `"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.0/bin/freecadcmd.exe" animo_v1_cube.py` |
| `cad/stl/animo_v1/` | Printable parts, regenerated by the macro on every run |
