# Animo

**A configurable actuator cube for cheap, printable toys and models — by Motcore.**

Animo is the small line of the Motcore family. Instead of wiring a servo into
every new design, you configure a cube once — which walls carry a motor, which
are blank, what sits on top — and then snap printed models onto it: a
puppet, a crane, a walker, a board game. One configuration, many models.

> Status: **early prototype**. v1 walls, gears and a test model have been
> printed and run on a servo; v2 (LEGO Technic faces) is the current design.

## The cube (v2)

- **56 mm cube (7 LEGO modules)**, four identical side walls, each a self-contained module.
- Each motor wall holds an **MG90D** (position) or an **MG90S 360°**
  (continuous) — same body, same module. A blank wall is the same plate
  without the servo.
- The four servos stand upright in a **pinwheel** round the cube's centre,
  leaving a square hole through the middle for the wiring.
- Each servo drives its wall's output through **two equal spur gears (1:1)**,
  so the output is on the **centre of the wall** and keeps the servo's whole
  travel.
- **Flat faces, LEGO Technic compatible.** The output is a Technic **cross
  hole** flush with the wall, and four Technic **pin holes** on the 8 mm grid
  (two under the output at ±8, one in an upper corner) hold the model on with standard friction pins: LEGO builds and printed
  models fit the same face. The cube parks the outputs at one angle before a
  model goes on, so nothing has to be aimed by hand. (v1 had a female D and
  four flush magnets: dropped, as loose magnets are a hazard.) Ages 7+.
- No bearings, no steel shafts: the output hub turns in a plain hole and is
  captured by the wall alone.
- **Swap a wall without opening the cube.** Each wall module (wall, servo,
  gears, hub) slides straight out through its window; two M2×6 countersunk
  screws in the face, on one diagonal of the grid, hold it to bosses on the
  two **identical decks**. Four M3×10 countersunk screws close the frame.
- The **brain** is a separate half cube that sits on the closed cube, held by
  two M3 screws: ESP32-C3, a charger + 5 V boost board, a 3 A rocker switch
  and a 1S LiPo. Boards snap into cradles; the battery hangs under the lid.
  Cube + brain stand 1.5 cubes tall.

## Files

| File | What |
|------|------|
| `cad/animo_v2_cube.py` | FreeCAD macro: the whole cube with LEGO Technic faces, parametric, with its own interference checks (LEGO pins and axle drawn as ghosts). Run it headless to read them: `"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.0/bin/freecadcmd.exe" animo_v2_cube.py` |
| `cad/stl/animo_v2/PETG/` | Printable parts, regenerated by the macro on every run (`MATERIAL` at the top, or `ANIMO_MATERIAL=PLA` headless for a PLA set) |
| `cad/animo_fit_coupon.py` | Fit coupons: round and square holes and pins, and LEGO Technic pin and cross holes to try real LEGO parts in. STLs in `cad/stl/coupon/` |
| `cad/animo_v1_cube.py` | v1, kept for reference: female D output and magnets |

### Printer notes (Creality Hi, Creality Print, 0.4 nozzle)

Turn **arc moves off** (small circles came out 0.2 mm oval) and use a **scarf
seam** (the plain seam's ~0.2 mm bump made fits scatter). Measured with those,
in PETG on a 6 mm feature: holes print ~0.27 small, outsides ~0.17 small;
press fit +0.05, sliding fit +0.25 (drawn hole minus drawn pin).
